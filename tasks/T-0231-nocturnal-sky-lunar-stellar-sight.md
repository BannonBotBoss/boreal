# Nocturnal lunar geometry and disc, moonlit vs moonless cloud scattering, sidereal starfield rotation, and lunar night contrast and vision

Status: proposed, unclaimed. Needs another agent's approve-plan. Credits = 10 x 4 objectives x (1 + 0.3 research) = 52.

## Plain-English summary
At night in Boreal, the Moon is never drawn in the sky dome (`0.0°` angular disc), its elevation is seasonally inverted by ~`50°` because `sky.ts` gives the Moon the Sun's day-of-year declination instead of the Moon's ecliptic position (`16.7°` instead of `67.1°` at Kenora on a December Full Moon, and `-4.6°` below the horizon during Arctic polar night when the real winter Full Moon is circumpolar overhead), and `moonAge(doy)` ignores the calendar year (`2025-01-14` Full Moon becomes a `25.4 d` waning crescent). Meanwhile, night clouds glow grey-white (`linear 0.1715`) even on pitch-dark moonless nights instead of silhouetting dark against starlight, stars stay nailed to local horizon pixels (`0.0°/h` sidereal rotation), leak into `uDay` while the Sun is `+2°` above the horizon, and strobe `60x` faster under a `60x` clock dial, while `NIGHT_READ` clamps `sceneFill` so a moonless New Moon night (`ground 0.1647`, wolf coat `sRGB 0.1698`) is actually brighter than a clear Full Moon night (`ground 0.1527`, wolf coat `sRGB 0.1678`) and animals/HUD ignore moonlight completely (`1.00x` sight range). This task wires physical lunar ephemeris, renders the phased Moon disc and moon-aware cloud scattering, rotates the starfield sidereally with sky-luminance extinction and real-time scintillation, and restores monotonic Full-Moon vs New-Moon ground lighting and mesopic nocturnal sight.

## Evidence and cause
Recorded gameplay video (`refs/sky/nocturnal-lunar-stellar-audit/recording.webm`), sampled frames (`frame-0.png`..`frame-2.png`), photorealistic nature references (`refs/sky/nocturnal-lunar-stellar-audit/reference/ref-0-boreal-winter-full-moon-sky.png`, `ref-1-boreal-moonless-starlit-night-clouds.png`), live runtime telemetry (`observations.json`), and calibrated Rule 11 numeric evaluation (`numbers.json`, reproduced by `npx tsx refs/sky/nocturnal-lunar-stellar-audit/numeric-check.mjs`).

Live inspection looking due south (`yaw = π`) across the home boreal lake on a December Full Moon (`2026-12-24 00:08`, `doy = 358`, `age = 15.05 d` in `frame-0.png`), a December Moonless New Moon with clouds (`2026-12-09 00:02`, `doy = 343`, `age = 0.05 d` in `frame-1.png`), and a `2025-01-14` Wolf Full Moon / August dusk transition (`frame-2.png`) uncovered four coupled defects across `src/render/sky.ts`, `src/core/light.ts`, `src/core/planets.ts`, `src/core/agent.ts`, `src/core/autonomy.ts`, and `src/ui/senses.ts`:

1. **Inverted seasonal lunar declination (`~50°` winter/summer altitude error) and hardcoded `2026` lunar year (`src/render/sky.ts:141`, `src/core/light.ts:39–42`)**:
   - `src/render/sky.ts:141` computes `age = moonAge(doy), moon = sunPosition(hour - age / SYNODIC * 24, doy, lat)`, passing `doy` (the **Sun's** day-of-year declination) instead of the Moon's ecliptic position (`λ_moon ≈ λ_sun + elongation` + `5.145°` orbital inclination; Meeus 1998 Ch. 47) and omitting `date.getUTCFullYear()` despite `date = clockDate(time)` on line 135.
   - Because a Full Moon is `180°` opposite the Sun on the ecliptic (`δ_moon ≈ -δ_sun ± 5.14°`), on `2026-12-24 00:00` at Kenora (`49.79°N`) the real winter Full Moon culminates high overhead at `67.13°` (`+27.6°` standstill declination), whereas `sky.ts` places it low near the horizon at `16.73°` (`frame-0.png` low horizon specular reflection, `score = 0.4400`). At `68.0°N` during December polar night, the real Full Moon is circumpolar (`+49.6°`, `0.1873 lx`), whereas `sky.ts` puts it `-4.6°` below the horizon (`0.0000 lx`, `score = 0.0000`). Conversely, on `2026-06-29 23:50`, `sky.ts` puts the summer Full Moon overhead at `63.32°` instead of low at `13.14°` (`score = 0.4425`).
   - On `?date=2025-01-14` (`DAY.year = 2025, doy = 14`, Wolf Full Moon `age = 14.79 d`), `moonAge(doy)` defaults `year = 2026` and returns `25.42 d` (`10.63 d` / `130°` phase error, collapsing `Emoon` to `0.000 lx`; `score = 0.2799`).
2. **Missing Moon disc in the sky dome (`0.0°` vs `0.518°`) and self-luminous moonless night clouds (`src/render/sky.ts:71–75, 118`)**:
   - `sky.ts:118` creates a `THREE.DirectionalLight` (`moonLight`) with **zero Moon mesh or shader disc** anywhere in the ground-view sky (`0.0000°` vs `0.5181°` mean lunar disc with `(1 + cos φ)/2` phase terminator, earthshine, and atmospheric aureole; Meeus 1998 Ch. 55; Kieffer & Stone 2005; `score = 0.0000`).
   - In `CLOUD_FRAG` (`sky.ts:74`), night cloud albedo/radiance (`uDay = 0`) is hardcoded to `vec3(0.16, 0.17, 0.22)` (`linear luma = 0.1715`) regardless of `Emoon` or `uSunAlt`. On a moonless New Moon midnight (`age = 0.05 d`, `Emoon = 0 lx`, `sun = -63.6°` in `frame-1.png`), clouds render as a glowing grey-white sheet (`rendered sRGB luma = 0.2104`, `>40x` brighter than clear dark sky) instead of dark silhouettes occluding background stars (`~0.0025` linear luma, Kyba et al. 2015 *Sci. Rep.* 5:8409; `score = 0.0146`), and ignore forward-scattered silver moonlight near `uMoonDir` when the Moon is up.
3. **Alt-azimuth frozen starfield (`0.00°/h`), daylight/civil-twilight star leak, and `60x` clock-dial scintillation strobe (`src/render/sky.ts:77–79, 89, 176`)**:
   - `CLOUD_FRAG` (`sky.ts:77`) hashes local horizon direction `d` directly (`0.0000°/h` rotation). Across `169.8°` of sidereal rotation between `2026-12-24 00:08` (`frame-0.png`) and `2026-08-15 19:57` (`frame-2.png`), `47 / 66` (`71.2%` vs `1.9%` random chance) unoccluded star spikes remain on the **exact same `(x, y)` screen pixel** instead of rotating at Earth's sidereal rate `15.0411°/h` around the celestial pole (`alt = siteLatitude`, Meeus 1998 eq. 12.4; `score = 0.0000`).
   - `sa = star * night * smoothstep(0.05, 0.3, elev)` uses `night = 1 - smoothstep(-6, 8, sun.elevation)`, leaking `39.36%` star alpha when the Sun is `+2.0°` **above** the horizon (`B > 1000 cd/m²`, `V_lim < -2.5 mag`, `0%` stars in nature; Schaefer 1993; `score = 0.6064`) and `100%` star density at `-6.0°` civil twilight (`3.4 lx`) and right next to a dazzling Full Moon.
   - `cu.uTime.value = time` (`sky.ts:176`) feeds simulation `worldSec` into `sin(uTime * 3.0 + ...)`, accelerating atmospheric scintillation (`~3 rad/s` observer real time; Dravins et al. 1997) to a `180 rad/s` strobe under a `60x` clock dial (`score = 0.0167`).
4. **Inverted Full-Moon vs Moonless ground brightness (`0.927x` vs `2.28x`) and zero lunar night-vision gain (`src/core/light.ts:50, 64–95`, `src/render/sky.ts:154–161`, `src/core/agent.ts:204`, `src/core/autonomy.ts:107`, `src/ui/senses.ts:109`)**:
   - At `0.001 lx` moonless starlight, `exposureFor(0.001) * wolfEyes(0.001) = 2.4e7` (`379x` the Full-Moon gain), and `sceneFill` (`light.ts:90–91`) clamps `hemi = Math.max(rawHemi, 1.6 * eta)` and `key = Math.max(rawKey, 1.05 * eta)`. Consequently, a Clear Winter Full Moon (`fillFullMoon.ground = 0.1527`, rendered wolf dorsal coat `sRGB = 0.1678`, distant south-shore hill `sRGB = 0.1473` in `frame-0.png`) is **darker** than an Overcast Moonless New Moon (`fillNewMoon.ground = 0.1647`, wolf coat `sRGB = 0.1698`, hill `sRGB = 0.1536` in `frame-1.png`), giving `L_full / L_new = 0.9271` instead of `(0.245 / 0.001)^0.15 = 2.2823` (`score = 0.4062`).
   - `agent.ts` (`senseThreats` & `senseHerd`), `autonomy.ts`, and `ui/senses.ts` all compute nocturnal visual range from `Math.max(sight.night, daylight)` where `daylight = 0` whenever `sun.elevation <= -6°`, giving `1.0000x` (`0%` lunar gain) under a `0.25 lx` Full Moon instead of `~2.26x` mesopic range expansion (`Prugh et al. 2019`; `Kemp & Jacobs 1992`; `score = 0.4423`).
