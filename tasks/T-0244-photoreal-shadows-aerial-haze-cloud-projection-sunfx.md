# Midnight daylight-white aerial horizon & weather-blind continuousTerrain haze, commented-out CSM shadowMapSize & dead PCSS / winter-leaf / wind shadow depth passes, zeroed & clamped 384m cloud shadows with squared Kasten-Czeplak attenuation, and SUNFX 1.6km depth-bleed & post-sunset/overcast god-rays

Status: proposed, unclaimed. Needs another agent's approve-plan. Credits = 10 x 4 objectives x (1 + 0.3 research) = 52.

## Plain-English summary
Four coupled optical, shadow-mapping, atmospheric-extinction, and post-processing defects prevent Boreal's lighting, shadows, haze, fog, and sunbeams from resembling the real world across day/night transitions, fog/rain storms, winter/windy canopies, and hilly horizons:
1. **At midnight, distant `continuousTerrain` hills glow bright daylight cream-white beneath a pitch-black starlit sky, and in dense fog or heavy rain trees vanish at `78 m` while the ground stays crystal-clear out to `18 km`**: `src/render/continuousTerrain.ts:235` disables `scene.fog` (`mat.fog = false`) and relies on `addAerialPerspective` (`src/render/aerial.ts:66–77`), which fades distant hills toward `AERIAL.uHorizon`. However, `src/render/sky.ts:150–151` sets `AERIAL.uHorizon.setRGB(0.78 + 0.14 * warm, 0.84 + 0.06 * warm, 0.92 - 0.08 * warm)` with **zero `light.day` or nocturnal luminance scaling** — so at `00:03` astronomical midnight (`sun.elevation = -26.2°`, `Esun = 0 lux`), `uHorizon` is `RGB(0.92, 0.90, 0.84)` (`linear luma = 0.8999`, brighter than noon `0.835`!), painting a bright white horizon band (`4.16×` brighter sRGB luma than the starlit sky in `frame-0.png`) beneath `fogNight = 0x0d1422` (`linear luma = 0.0070`). Meanwhile, `sky.ts:185,200` shrinks `scene.fog` visibility to `77.6 m` in dense radiation fog (Kunkel 1984) and `1,927 m` in `25 mm/h` rain, whereas `aerial.ts:24` hardcodes clear-air `BETA_M = 2.2e-4` (`V = 17,781.8 m`) and never reads `weatherAt` — making shrubs, trees, and rocks vanish at `78 m` while `continuousTerrain` ridgelines remain `229×` clearer all the way to the horizon (`frame-1.png`)!
2. **`CSM` `shadowMapSize` is accidentally commented out (allocating `4×` shadow texels on Low/XR), `#ifdef PCSS` is dead across all shaders (`0.00 m` solar-disc penumbra), leafless winter birch/aspen cast giant solid summer-foliage cone shadows on snow, and wind-swaying crowns cast rigid frozen shadows**: On `src/render/photoreal.ts:92`, `shadowMapSize: tier.resScale >= 1 ? 2048 : 1024` sits inside a trailing `//` comment (`maxFar: 110, // ≈ the LOD-1 range... shadowMapSize: ...`), forcing `2048×2048` (`4,194,304` texels/cascade instead of `1,048,576`) even on `Medium`, `Low`, and Quest `XR_CAPS`. `quality.ts:17,29–30` and `photoreal.ts:108,130,157` define `PCSS = ''` and recompile shader variants (and `main.ts:812` benchmarks `ultra-without-pcss`), yet **`0` shaders read `#ifdef PCSS`** (`0.0000 m` solar `0.533°` penumbra softening). Worse, `trees.ts`, `trees/conifer.ts`, `flora.ts`, and `grass.ts` attach **no `customDepthMaterial`**, so Three.js's shadow pass ignores `if (decid * uOff > nn) discard` (`trees.ts:106`) — causing leafless winter `trembling-aspen`, `birch`, and `balsam-poplar` (`uOff = 1.0`) to cast huge solid summer-cone shadows across January snow (`frame-2.png`) — and ignores `uWind` vertex sway (`0 / 4` vegetation casters).
3. **`CLOUD.uCloudTex` stays `0.000` under `64–92%` planetary cloud cover, `100%` of Kenora daylight hours project outside the `384 m` `ClampToEdgeWrapping` box, and `addCloudShadow` squares Kasten–Czeplak overcast attenuation (`0.25² = 0.0625`)**: `photoreal.ts:83` populates `uCloudTex` solely from `grid.qc`, ignoring `PlanetSim` `clim.cloud` (`weather.ts:457`) and `sky.clouds`; because `initWeather` (`weather.ts:212`) zeroes `grid.qc`, `uCloudTex` is `0` across every texel even under `64.1%`–`92.2%` cloud cover (`observations.json`). Even when `grid.qc > 0`, `src/render/pbr.ts:124` projects `(1000.0 - vCloudW.y) * uSunWorld.xz / uSunWorld.y` onto a `uCloudBox.z = 384 m` (`±192 m`) `ClampToEdgeWrapping` texture (`photoreal.ts:84` also cancels `NX * (384 / NX) === 384`); at Kenora (`49.79° N`), max annual solar elevation is `63.65°` (`1000 / tan(63.65°) = 495.3 m > 192 m`), so **`100%` of daylight hours all year clamp to the texture border**! Furthermore, `sky.ts:142` already multiplies `Esun` by Kasten & Czeplak (`1 - 0.75 C^3.4 = 0.25`), so `pbr.ts:125` multiplying `directDiffuse`/`directSpecular` by `1 - 0.75 c^3.4` again squares overcast attenuation to `0.0625` while omitting `conifer.ts`, `ROCK_MATERIAL`, `LOG_MATERIAL`, and animals.
4. **Post-processing `SUNFX` (`src/render/post.ts:91,99,142`) treats every solid hill beyond `1,600 m` as unoccluded sky (shining god-rays through solid ridges), ignores overcast cloud attenuation, and runs down to `-2.87°` below the horizon after sunset**: With `camera.near = 0.2 m` and `camera.far >= 8,000 m` (`buildScene.ts:115,538`), non-linear perspective depth reaches `0.9999` at $z = \frac{f \cdot n}{f - 0.9999(f - n)} = 1,600.03\text{ m}$. Consequently, `step(.9999, texture2D(tDepth, ...).x)` (`post.ts:99`) classifies **all solid terrain from `1.6 km` to `8+ km` as glowing sky**, shining radial god-rays right through solid distant hills (`frame-3.png`) and disabling contact shadows beyond `1.6 km` (`d0 < .9999`). In addition, `buildScene.ts:561` passes normalised `sky.sunLight.color` (ignoring `Esun` and `wq.cloud`), `post.ts:99` smears border texels via `clamp(uv, 0., 1.)` when `uSunUv` is off-screen, and `post.ts:142` (`fx.sunDir.y > -.05`) keeps firing contact shadows and god-rays down to `-2.87°` after sunset when `Esun = 0 lux`!
