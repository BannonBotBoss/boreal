## Plain-English summary
Four connected population-scale, nocturnal-ground-stain, animal-lifetime, and sensory-stream bugs break how animals transform with time, age through life, and perceive the world:
1. **Speeding up time draws frozen animal statues right on top of their own population patches, and shrinks hare populations toward deer numbers**: When time accelerates into group or ecosystem speeds, every wild hare, deer, bear, eagle, and beaver stays `100%` solid on screen right on top of its population ground patch (counting every animal twice), while the population math resets every frame and pushes snowshoe hares toward `1` per square kilometre instead of `1,000`.
2. **Population ground patches float out into mid-air off hills and lakes, glow neon olive-green in pitch-black winter midnight, and strip fur/shadow shaders when fading**: Each population patch is a flat horizontal circle up to `110 m` wide that juts metres into the air off sloped shores, shines brightly across dark `34 cm` winter snow at midnight, and strips custom fur/shadow shaders whenever a body fades.
3. **One year on the time dial ages your animal by `12` years into an immortal `122`-year-old wolf, while switching animals resets to a static file with a submerged hare and missing packmate**: Turning the dial at `"1 year / sec"` ages your body `12×` faster than cosmic time and pups without ever triggering natural old-age limits, and switching animals reads four frozen coordinates (placing the hare below lake water level, omitting the second wolf packmate, and forgetting where your previous animal walked).
4. **The embodied sensory buffer is disconnected from live play, ignores `-35 °C` winter cold and underwater breath-holding, and drops eye sightings whenever sounds and scents overlap**: Live play never runs the packed sensory buffer (`Data (U)` lists it under `NOT IN RUST`), cold air below `-10 °C` produces zero cold stress, and the `8`-slot sound limit on the senses panel blocks eye icons whenever `8` sounds and scents are already floating.
